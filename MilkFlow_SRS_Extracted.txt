1. Introduction

1.1 Purpose

This Software Requirements Specification defines the functional, non-functional, data, security, interface, operational, and acceptance requirements for MilkFlow, a multi-tenant digital dairy management platform. The system is intended to replace fragmented manual registers, spreadsheets, messaging-based workflows, and informal payment tracking with a controlled operational system covering customer onboarding, subscriptions, daily delivery, billing, payments, inventory, notifications, auditability, analytics, and governed AI assistance.

1.2 Product Vision

MilkFlow shall provide one authoritative business workflow from customer relationship creation through subscription, expected delivery, actual delivery, exception handling, invoice generation, payment reconciliation, and audit. The system shall prioritize correctness, tenant isolation, traceability, operational usability, and safe handling of financial and personal data.

Customer onboarding -> Invitation -> Activation -> Subscription -> Expected delivery      -> Actual delivery -> Adjustment/Dispute -> Billable quantity      -> Invoice -> Payment -> Receipt -> Audit / Reporting

1.3 Scope of This SRS

This SRS covers the complete target platform, including the operational portal for dairy owners and staff, the customer portal, platform administration, authentication, role and tenant authorization, data management, delivery routes, pricing and subscription versioning, billing, payment gateway integration, inventory/day closing, notifications, QR delivery verification, analytics, governed AI, observability, security controls, privacy controls, disaster recovery, deployment, testing, and acceptance.

1.4 Intended Audience

Project owner / dairy operator: validates business processes and acceptance criteria.

Project guide / faculty reviewer: evaluates scope, requirements quality, traceability, and engineering depth.

Developers: implement frontend, backend, database, security, integrations, and automation.

QA / security reviewers: derive test cases from functional, non-functional, and security requirements.

Operations / deployment team: deploy, monitor, back up, recover, and maintain the service.

Future client users: farmers, managers, delivery personnel, accountants, support staff, and customers.

1.5 Reference Basis

This SRS is based on the project repository structure and documentation, the project’s dairy-operations/domain implementation plan, the existing relational data model, API and security specifications, and the project’s stated production-readiness objectives. The repository presently describes a Next.js App Router application, PostgreSQL/Neon persistence, Prisma, role-based access, signed sessions, payment webhooks, a hash-chained audit mechanism, and customer/farmer portals. These statements are treated as design/source inputs; implementation claims are not treated as proof of production readiness.

2. Overall Description

2.1 Product Perspective

MilkFlow is a cloud-deployed, multi-tenant web application. A tenant represents a dairy business or operating organization. Within a tenant, one or more owners/managers/staff manage products, prices, customers, subscriptions, routes, deliveries, invoices, payments, inventory, and operational requests. Customers use an invitation-activated portal tied to their assigned dairy relationship. A platform-level SuperAdmin can monitor tenant health and platform governance without becoming a participant in tenant business operations.

2.3 Core Design Principles

PostgreSQL is the authoritative source of truth for persistent business state.

Farmer/owner/admin controls the customer relationship, pricing, subscription and financial rules; customers submit requests through the governed portal.

Every protected request is evaluated for authentication, role, tenant, and resource ownership.

Historical financial and delivery facts are not silently overwritten; corrections use explicit adjustment or correction workflows.

AI recommendations are advisory and cannot directly mutate financial or delivery state.

All high-value mutations are auditable and suitable for forensic review.

External integrations are isolated behind provider adapters and shall not become the system of record.

2.7 Constraints

The system shall not depend on an in-memory singleton for authoritative business data in production.

Production deployment shall not use demo persona authentication or public customer self-registration.

Financial state transitions shall occur within transactional boundaries.

External payment webhook verification secrets shall be supplied by environment/secret management and shall fail closed when missing.

The browser shall not be trusted to provide tenant, farmer, role, or financial ownership values.

The application shall remain usable on mobile devices for delivery operations.

The platform must support incremental replacement of integrations without changing the core domain model.

3. Functional Requirements

3.1 Authentication & Identity

FR-AUTH-001 [Must]: The system shall authenticate a user using a real registered credential (phone or email plus password, according to tenant configuration) stored as a secure password hash in PostgreSQL.  Source: Requirements baseline.

FR-AUTH-002 [Must]: The system shall reject authentication attempts with invalid credentials using a generic failure response that does not unnecessarily reveal account existence.  Source: Requirements baseline.

FR-AUTH-003 [Must]: The production login endpoint shall not accept a demo user identifier, persona selector, or preset account as an authentication factor.  Source: Requirements baseline.

FR-AUTH-004 [Must]: The system shall create a cryptographically protected session after successful authentication.  Source: Requirements baseline.

FR-AUTH-005 [Must]: The session shall bind the authenticated user to a tenant and role, and shall contain or permit secure resolution of the user’s business identity (farmer/staff/customer).  Source: Requirements baseline.

FR-AUTH-006 [Must]: The system shall reject expired, malformed, unsigned, or tampered session tokens.  Source: Requirements baseline.

FR-AUTH-007 [Must]: Session cookies shall be HttpOnly, Secure in production, and use an appropriate SameSite policy.  Source: Requirements baseline.

FR-AUTH-008 [Must]: The system shall support logout and invalidate/expire the active session.  Source: Requirements baseline.

FR-AUTH-009 [Must]: The system shall allow an authenticated user to change their password after verifying the existing credential.  Source: Requirements baseline.

FR-AUTH-010 [Must]: The system shall support a secure password-reset flow using a short-lived, single-use proof (OTP or reset link) without storing raw reset secrets.  Source: Requirements baseline.

FR-AUTH-011 [Must]: The system shall rate-limit login and password-reset endpoints and record repeated suspicious authentication activity.  Source: Requirements baseline.

FR-AUTH-012 [Must]: The system shall not select a user, farmer or tenant by “first matching record” as a fallback for missing session identity.  Source: Requirements baseline.

FR-AUTH-013 [Must]: The system shall invalidate or rotate sessions after security-sensitive credential changes according to the security policy.  Source: Requirements baseline.

FR-CUST-007 [Must]: The invitation flow shall not permit the invitee to select or change the owning tenant/farmer relationship.  Source: Requirements baseline.

FR-CUST-008 [Must]: The system shall expose invitation delivery channel and invitation status to authorized tenant staff.  Source: Requirements baseline.

FR-CUST-009 [Must]: The system shall support re-invitation/rotation without invalidating the entire customer record.  Source: Requirements baseline.

4. Business Rules and Domain Invariants

4.1 Customer Relationship Rules

A customer is created by the dairy business, not by public sign-up.

Customer activation establishes credentials; it does not establish ownership.

Customer personal-profile edits are limited to allowed personal/contact fields.

A customer cannot self-select a farmer, tenant, product price or billing rule.

Ownership transfer is a governed domain event; history remains attached to the original business context.

4.2 Subscription & Delivery Rules

Expected delivery is derived from the effective subscription version and schedule.

Actual delivery is a separate operational fact.

Price on a delivery record is locked for that business date; current catalog price cannot rewrite the past.

SKIPPED and NOT_DELIVERED normally produce zero billable quantity; other states follow explicit quantity rules.

Disputed deliveries remain traceable to the original record and are adjusted through dispute/credit/debit workflows.

4.3 Closing Rules

Finalized days are immutable for ordinary delivery edits.

Closed months are immutable for ordinary invoice rewrites.

Corrections require explicit adjustment objects and authorized approval.

Closing operations shall be transactionally serialized for a tenant/farmer/date or tenant/month key.

4.4 Financial Rules

Exactly one logical invoice per customer and billing month/year is the baseline rule.

Payment transaction reference is unique and is the idempotency anchor for gateway replay protection.

Outstanding amount shall never become negative unless credit-balance behavior is explicitly modeled.

Financial amounts and quantities are server-computed from authoritative records.

4.5 AI Authority Rule

AI may recommend forecasts, customer-health signals, or operational actions but shall never autonomously create or modify deliveries, invoices, payments, customer ownership, pricing, or other financial/domain state. Any AI suggestion that changes business state must pass a human approval step through the same authorized domain service used by normal application actions.

7. Security Requirements

7.1 Security Objectives

Prevent unauthorized access and privilege escalation.

Prevent cross-tenant information disclosure or mutation.

Prevent financial replay/double posting.

Preserve auditability and detect tampering.

Minimize exposure of credentials, PII and payment metadata.

Provide defense in depth against brute force, injection and malicious payloads.

SEC-001 [Must]: Passwords shall never be stored in plaintext.  Source: Requirements baseline.

SEC-002 [Must]: Password hashing shall use a modern memory-hard KDF such as scrypt/Argon2id with unique cryptographic salt and appropriate work factor.  Source: Requirements baseline.

SEC-003 [Must]: Password verification shall use constant-time comparison for derived secrets where applicable.  Source: Requirements baseline.

SEC-004 [Must]: Session tokens shall be cryptographically protected (e.g., HMAC-signed or securely opaque server-side sessions).  Source: Requirements baseline.

SEC-005 [Must]: Authorization shall enforce role, tenant and resource ownership on every protected API.  Source: Requirements baseline.

SEC-006 [Must]: The system shall reject attempts to access or mutate records from another tenant.  Source: Requirements baseline.

SEC-007 [Must]: The system shall reject customer attempts to mutate protected ownership/business fields.  Source: Requirements baseline.

SEC-008 [Must]: All SQL access shall use parameterized queries/ORM parameters; dynamically concatenated SQL from user input is prohibited.  Source: Requirements baseline.

SEC-009 [Must]: The application shall apply input validation and contextual output encoding to mitigate injection and XSS risks.  Source: Requirements baseline.

SEC-010 [Must]: Security headers shall include appropriate CSP, HSTS, X-Content-Type-Options and frame restrictions in production.  Source: Requirements baseline.

SEC-011 [Must]: Authentication and high-risk API endpoints shall be rate-limited using a distributed mechanism where horizontal scaling exists.  Source: Requirements baseline.

SEC-012 [Must]: Secrets shall be loaded from managed environment/secret stores and shall not be committed to source control.  Source: Requirements baseline.

SEC-013 [Must]: Payment webhooks shall fail closed when required verification secrets are missing or invalid.  Source: Requirements baseline.

SEC-014 [Must]: Operational audit logs shall avoid sensitive password/reset-token/payment-secret content.  Source: Requirements baseline.

SEC-015 [Must]: The application shall implement CSRF protection where cookie-authenticated state-changing requests are vulnerable, using same-site policy plus explicit CSRF strategy as appropriate.  Source: Requirements baseline.

SEC-016 [Must]: The platform shall implement secure error handling that avoids leaking stack traces, SQL statements or sensitive identifiers to clients.  Source: Requirements baseline.

SEC-017 [Must]: Security-relevant events such as repeated authentication failures, privilege changes and blocked malicious requests shall be observable.  Source: Requirements baseline.

Appendix E - Requirement Quality Rules

Every SHALL requirement must be observable/testable.

Requirements must be implementation-independent unless the implementation constraint is itself mandatory.

No requirement should depend on fabricated data or demo users.

Historical facts should be immutable by default; corrections should be explicit.

Security requirements apply to every API, not only UI routes.

External providers must be treated as untrusted dependencies whose responses are validated.

AI output is never equivalent to authoritative business state.
